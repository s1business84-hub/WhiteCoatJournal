BriTrain · TSRHRT

28–31 August 2026 · Oxford · IFMSA-linked training

Personal Reflection

BriTrain participants
Graduation presentation
A

Description of the Activity

What happened — the factual account

From 28–31 August 2026, I participated in BriTrain, an international IFMSA-linked training in Oxford, completing the Training Sexual and Reproductive Human Rights Trainers (TSRHRT) pathway. Alongside students from different backgrounds, I explored sexual and reproductive health and rights, intersectionality, LGBTQ+ health, gender-based violence, comprehensive sexuality education, trainers’ ethics and facilitation. Interactive activities regularly placed us in different groups. For graduation, my partner and I jointly designed and delivered an HIV/AIDS session, including its lesson plan, slides and activities.

B

Self-Reflection

Gibbs Reflective Cycle

Step 1Description

The workshops introduced perspectives I had not previously explored in depth, particularly experiences and healthcare concerns relating to LGBTQ+ people. Working with participants from different backgrounds encouraged discussion and reflection. Our graduation session then challenged us to apply what we had learned about collaborative, respectful health education.

Step 2Feelings

I was curious but initially uncertain when discussions touched on viewpoints that differed from my personal beliefs. I realised my limited knowledge might shape my assumptions. Hearing other perspectives helped me approach the subject with greater understanding, without feeling that I had to abandon my own beliefs. Preparing HIV/AIDS education also made me conscious of the responsibility to avoid language that reinforces stigma.

Step 3Evaluation

A strength was my willingness to listen and contribute despite unfamiliar ideas. Planning and delivering the graduation session jointly gave me practical experience of shared responsibility. However, I recognised that I could confuse my own familiarity with a topic with how confidently I could discuss it. I needed to consider whether our activities welcomed learners with different perspectives.

Step 4Analysis

BriTrain changed my understanding of collaboration. Working effectively with people who think differently does not require agreement on every issue; it requires curiosity, mutual respect and a shared commitment to the task. Learning about LGBTQ+ experiences showed me why healthcare professionals must distinguish personal beliefs from professional obligations to treat every patient fairly and without judgement. It also revealed how limited knowledge can contribute to assumptions or leave patients’ concerns unheard. My partner and I applied similar principles when designing our HIV/AIDS session: scientific accuracy mattered, but so did accessible explanations, participation and sensitivity to stigma. I came to see leadership as creating space for different viewpoints while remaining accountable for a shared educational outcome.

Step 5Conclusion

The most meaningful outcome was greater openness to understanding experiences unlike my own, combined with practical experience of collaborative teaching. I learned that respectful teamwork and inclusive healthcare depend on listening as much as speaking.

Step 6Action Plan

In future group work, I will invite perspectives different from mine, agree on shared objectives and check that everyone can contribute. I will strengthen my knowledge of inclusive sexual-health communication using reliable medical guidance, seek feedback on my language and teamwork, and include a brief check of learners’ understanding in future teaching sessions.
