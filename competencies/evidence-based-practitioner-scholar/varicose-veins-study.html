Varicose Veins in the UAE

Published 11 September 2026 · Cross-sectional epidemiological study
A

Description of the Activity

Publication and research contribution

I co-authored an original epidemiological research article in Frontiers in Epidemiology, published on 11 September 2026: “A survey of self-reported varicose veins with prior clinical diagnosis among adults residing in the United Arab Emirates: a cross-sectional study examining associated factors.” The UAE-wide study recruited 1,043 eligible adults and examined associations between self-reported clinically diagnosed varicose veins and demographic, lifestyle and medical factors.

The sample reported a 15.05% prevalence of prior clinical diagnosis, with age, BMI, prolonged standing and family history associated with higher odds. The authors explicitly characterised the findings as preliminary and non-generalizable because convenience sampling was used.

B

Scholarly Reflection

Evidence-based practice and limitations

1Description

The project used a cross-sectional survey across all seven emirates, expert review of the questionnaire and SPSS analysis to estimate prevalence and examine associations.

2Feelings

Contributing to the study strengthened my interest in population-level clinical questions and in the responsibilities that accompany interpreting and communicating scientific findings.

3Evaluation

The study addressed a UAE research gap and used vascular-surgery and community-medicine expert review. Its convenience sample and lack of formal psychometric validation limit generalizability and require cautious interpretation.

4Analysis

Publication demonstrated the progression from a clinical question to survey design, ethical recruitment, analysis and evidence communication. It also reinforced that statistical association does not establish causation and that sample representativeness shapes the confidence with which results can be translated into practice.

5Conclusion

The project developed my understanding of epidemiological methods, study limitations and responsible scientific scholarship. It showed how preliminary research can identify priorities while still requiring validated, probability-based follow-up studies.

6Action Plan

I will continue developing methodological and critical-appraisal skills, seek training in psychometric and probabilistic sampling methods, and critically report uncertainty when translating research findings into clinical or public-health conclusions.
